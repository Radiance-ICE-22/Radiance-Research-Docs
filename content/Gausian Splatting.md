# Pipeline
1. Pick a random training image
2. Render current Gaussians → get predicted image
3. Compute **loss** comparing predicted vs ground truth
4. Compute **gradients** of loss w.r.t Gaussian parameters
5. Use **adaptive step / optimizer** to update all visible Gaussians
6. Optional: split/prune Gaussians if error/density criteria are met
7. Move to next iteration (next image)

# Steps (In learn openCV)
- Projecting 3D Gaussians onto a 2D plane.
- Optimizing Gaussians to accurately capture the scene.
- Adaptive density control of 3D Gaussians
- Rendering 3D Gaussians onto a 2D image plane.
- Leveraging Spherical Harmonics to make 3DGS view-dependent.

# 3D Gausians
- $\Sigma$ ---> covariance
$\Sigma =\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{xx} & \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{xz} \\\sigma_{yx} & \sigma_{yy} & \sigma_{yz} \\\sigma_{zx} & \sigma_{zy} & \sigma_{zz}\end{bmatrix}$
- $\mu$  ----> Mean
	$\mu =\begin{bmatrix}\mu_x \\\mu_y \\\mu_z\end{bmatrix}$
-  3D Gaussian PDF
$\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x} \mid \mu, \Sigma)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}} |\Sigma|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mu)\right)$

- $\alpha$ ----> blending
	Alpha blending is ==a computer graphics technique for compositing images or objects with partial transparency by using an **alpha channel**, a fourth color component (besides Red, Green, Blue) that defines a pixel's opacity (from fully transparent to fully opaque)==.
- These primitives are differentiable and easy to be rasterized.

- Above is the optimization pipeline of 3D gaussian splatting.
- It takes a sparse point cloud as input (generated from SFM) along side camera position and the corresponding image captured from that position.
- Each point is used to initialized 3D gausian.
- Apart from these there are other gausians that are being initialized at other places in the volume randomly. 
- After that --> **Adaptive density control** is applied responsible for **pruning** and **densification** of the gaussians.
- After that's done, we sample a camera position and its respective image and project the guassians in a 2D image plane. 
	- Previously mentioned projection method and a tile based rasterizer is used for rendering the image.
	- This image and the image associated with the camera position are used to calculate the loss.
![[Pasted image 20260107225125.png]]
---
- Here author used SGD (Adam) for optimization, loss function is L1 loss combine with a D-SSIM term.
![[Pasted image 20260108230448.png]]
- **Differential structural similarity index measure** --- D-SSIM
	Image similarity measure that takes ==luminance, Contrast== and ==structural information== in to consideration.
- **SSIM** is better than other methods such as **MSE(mean squared error)** and **PSNR (Peak signal-to-noise ratio)** as they tend to estimate absolute error.
- The optimization tries to optimize 4 learnable parameters,
	- Gaussian position (M)
	- Cavariance (S)
	- Colors (C) ---> ~={red}Function of spherical harmonic(SH) parameters=~
	- Opacities (A) or transparency
